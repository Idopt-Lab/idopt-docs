## What you are running

Open [sandbox.idoptlab.com](https://sandbox.idoptlab.com). It compares a county
round trip using driving, air, train and bus. Its air calculations include saved
RAM network routes where Grant's code inserts them into the air matrices; RAM
is not a separate fifth calibrated choice. RAM means regional air mobility. This is a research calculation,
not a ticket search. Cloudflare serves the website and Modal runs Python; your
laptop does not need to stay on.

Grant's trip-cost rules and mode-choice equations remain in place. The predictor
package supplies transport prices and times through adapters. The website
recalculates county results against a **fixed saved RAM network**; it does not
rerun the full fleet/network optimization for each request.

## Run the demo

1. Choose the Blacksburg-area origin and Washington, DC destination.
2. Run with **Bus / rail inputs → Grant's original baseline** first.
3. Switch to **Our predictors — recorded services**, enter departure and return
   dates, and enable estimated rail fares if you want an Amtrak price estimate.
4. Run again. Compare mode costs, times and shares, then inspect the source and
   schedule warnings. Export the scenario JSON to reproduce your settings.

Your station-distance limits determine which nearby stops are candidates; they
are not maximum train or bus trip distances. If no supported service connects
the selected candidates, that option remains unavailable. Acela is not invented
from Roanoke simply because you selected it.

## JSON settings

Add this field to an existing exported scenario. It is a fragment, not a complete
scenario file:

```json
{
  "ground_transport": {
    "source": "predictor",
    "departure_date": "2026-11-17",
    "return_date": "2026-11-18",
    "allow_rail_fare_estimates": true,
    "schedule_delay_minutes": 30
  }
}
```

Omit `ground_transport`, or use `{"source":"legacy"}`, to retain Grant's
original baseline. Predictor inputs currently require scenario year 2026 and
legacy data year 2016. Both dates are required; the return cannot precede the
departure. The schedule-delay setting is minutes **per direction**, from 0 to
1,440, and defaults to zero. Rail fare estimates default to disabled.

The repository's `schemas/sandbox-scenario.schema.json` defines the full input
contract. Use the exported scenario rather than constructing county identifiers
by hand. JSON and YAML runners share the scenario validation. Local predictor
bus/rail runs also require `--current-air` and the private study inputs.

## What changes between the two settings

| Input | Grant's original baseline | Predictor option |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bus | Original study station, fare and time tables | Virginia Breeze stops, published fares and dated single-bus services |
| Rail | Original study station, fare and time tables | Amtrak/Acela single-train schedule medians; fares only with explicit estimate opt-in |
| Station access | Original county-to-station tables | County-centroid-to-current-stop straight-line distance, with Grant's existing access speed and cost rules |
| Waiting | Original study values | Grant's processing time plus your chosen schedule delay per direction |
| Study equations | Original cost, lodging and mode-choice rules | Same rules; supplied transport inputs change |

The predictor option does not use generic bus heuristics to invent missing
services. It checks outbound and return services separately. Intermediate stops
are already part of scheduled riding time; no additional transfers are planned.
Station access is not a newly routed road journey. Reduce the station-distance
limits if the candidate search exceeds 100 station pairs per mode.

## Read the output correctly

County results are **round trips in the legacy 2016-dollar study cost basis**.
They can include station access, waiting and lodging, not just tickets. Predictor
fare assumptions are converted from 2026 dollars to the study basis before
calculation. Do not compare a county cost total directly with a one-way ticket.

The separate [predictor quote API](/air-travel-model/api/quotes) returns one-way
prices and minutes. Its verbose response explains individual time and price
components. The survey and sandbox share that engine, but their boundaries differ:

| Endpoint | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `/api/quotes` on either website | Terminal-to-terminal mode estimates, optional journey allowances and explanations |
| `/api/plan` on either website | One-way coordinate-based survey journey comparison |
| `/api/run` on the sandbox | County round-trip study with Grant's cost and mode-choice rules |

Commercial airfare is a typical whole-market estimate, not the cheapest ticket
and not a separately priced itinerary through a particular connecting airport.
RAM quotes are hypothetical, with a configurable 300-mile single-leg limit by
default; they do not manufacture multi-hop service to California.

## What happens after the rail feed expires

The hosted service refreshes Amtrak schedules daily at 10:00 UTC. The bundled
October 18 snapshot covered October 18–24; the October 19 refresh advanced the
window to October 19–25. The actual loaded dates are returned in `rail_schedule`.

Outside that window, a known route can still provide a **last-known schedule
estimate**. That does not confirm a train on your requested date. Even inside
the window, the model returns a schedule median, not a reserved departure.
Failures retain the last good data and the scheduled job retries the next day.
Serving processes check the shared cache at most every five minutes.

This refresh updates schedules and stations, not rail ticket prices. Amtrak
fares remain aggregate revenue-per-passenger-mile estimates. Virginia Breeze
has a separate dated feed; its out-of-window dates return unavailable and its
feed is not covered by the Amtrak refresh job.

## What is still old or estimated

- The demand base is still the 2016 county trip tables. Woods & Poole CEDDS 2025
  projections grow that base to a 2026 scenario; they are not observed 2026 trips.
- The default bus/rail baseline and commercial schedule-delay tables retain
  legacy inputs. Choosing predictor bus/rail does not replace every study table.
- Cached road coverage is bounded; uncovered pairs can retain legacy inputs.
- Rail fares are estimates, flight fares are market averages, and neither is
  a live booking quote. Passing integration tests does not establish price accuracy.

Licensed Woods & Poole archives and normalized values must remain outside public
repositories and documentation. Local runners also need the lab's study assets;
installing the Python package alone does not supply them.
